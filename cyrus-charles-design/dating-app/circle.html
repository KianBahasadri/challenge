What you’re seeing

Everyone in your circle fits, because you haven’t narrowed anything. Move a slider or turn on a requirement and the count will drop.

The list is already made

This only works if Meta is the one making it, because Meta already has the graph. Instagram, your contacts, the rest of the account. On the backend, that data becomes a list of eligible people in your circle. You don’t go looking for strangers, and nobody fills out a dating profile to get on the list.

You describe a type

Height and income are sliders: drag the minimum and the pool moves with your finger. Everything else is a requirement you can turn on. School, work, how they stay active, what they’re into, how they live. Those facts are already tied together. Medicine and law come with the degree. Taller people are the ones in certain sports. The job is what the income follows. Each requirement you turn on has to be true. Turn it off and it isn’t.

The number is the pool

You don’t browse the list to find out if your type is realistic. The phone tells you the exact count of people in your circle who pass. Add a requirement and the pool shrinks. Take one off and it grows. Each dot is one person: bright if they still fit, dim if they dropped out.

Then the week is over

When the count feels right, you look at who is left and ask two of them. If one says yes, you have one date. If both say yes, you have two. Two is the cap for the week. You don’t get a substitute, and you don’t keep scrolling.
